BotBattle

Program a robot in a small language of its own. Then find out whose is best.

Play

What it is

A spiritual successor to Robot Battle (1993) and Robocode (2000). You write a robot's brain in RoboScript — an event-driven language with no punctuation to get wrong — and watch it fight in an arena. Nobody writes JavaScript, so a script from a stranger is safe to run. 

-- Sweeps for a target, then chases it down.
name "Hunter"
chassis tank

on start
  turret.sweep 45
  drive forward 70
end

on sense robot
  set name = "hunting"
  turret.aim at event.bearing
  fire 3
  turn body by event.bearing
end

on hit by bullet
  -- The bearing points back at whoever shot you.
  turn body by event.bearing + 90
  drive forward 100
end

Why it works the way it does

  • Every screen sees the same match. A battle is fully determined by its manifest — the scripts, a seed, the arena — because there is no human input once it starts. So peers exchange manifests, not game state, and each one plays the whole battle locally. Someone losing connection halfway through changes nothing.
  • A manifest is already a replay. Keeping your battle history costs a few kilobytes, and every past fight can be watched again.
  • Two ways to move. Tracks pivot on the spot but are slow; wheels are fast but carry a turning circle they cannot escape. The circle falls out of the physics rather than being a rule.
  • Robotics or biology. The same simulation, two vocabularies. chassis tank and body ciliate compile to byte-identical bytecode, so the theme is wording and art and never an advantage.

Modes

  • Workshop — build a robot, keep every version, and measure whether a change actually helped.
  • Arena — everyone's robot at once, all against all.
  • Tournament — one against one until a single robot is left.
  • Pair Program — build one robot together, with chat.
  • Trade — swap copies of your robots, with permission.
